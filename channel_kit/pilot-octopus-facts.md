# Fact sheet: 5 octopus facts that sound impossible

## Audience promise

Explain how an octopus's circulation, movement, senses, and camouflage make its
body radically different from ours.

## Verified claims

1. Cephalopods have three hearts: two branchial hearts push oxygen-depleted
   blood through the gills, while the systemic heart sends oxygenated blood
   through the body.
   Source: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
2. Their blood is blue because hemocyanin, the oxygen-carrying molecule, uses
   copper; humans use an iron-based oxygen-transport system.
   Source: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
3. Bottom-dwelling octopuses generally reserve jet propulsion for escape and
   usually walk across the seafloor using their arms.
   Source: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
4. Red octopuses can rapidly change color and alter skin texture to camouflage.
   Source: https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/animals-a-to-z/red-octopus
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
5. A red octopus has chemical and texture receptors around its suckers and uses
   touch and smell when finding food.
   Source: https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/animals-a-to-z/red-octopus
   Accessed: 2026-08-25

## Context and caveats

- Claims 4 and 5 are specifically documented for the red octopus.
- Do not claim that every octopus species behaves identically.

## Words and claims to avoid

- Avoid the disputed oversimplification that an octopus's main heart always
  stops for the entire time it swims.
- Do not call hemocyanin universally more efficient than hemoglobin.

## Visual ideas

- Gill/body circulation diagram, crawling octopus, short escape jet, close-up
  sucker footage, and a before/after camouflage sequence.
